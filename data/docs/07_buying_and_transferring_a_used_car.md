# Buying and transferring a used car
Source URLs:
https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/selling-deregistering/transfer-ownership.html
https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/improving-motor-vehicle-ownership-transfer-process/
https://www.motorist.sg/article/910/buying-a-car-from-a-direct-owner-these-are-the-paperwork-you-will-need-to-complete (practical steps, not official)
Date notes were written: 2026-11-08 (summarised in own words; the practical details such as the fee and the acceptance window come from a motoring guide and should be checked on OneMotoring)

Two ways to buy: You can buy a used car from a motor dealer or from a direct owner. LTA advises using a reliable dealer, and for extra assurance a dealer accredited under CaseTrust-SVTA.
How ownership transfer works: The seller starts the transfer on OneMotoring and the buyer accepts it. Both use SingPass with two-factor authentication. There is a two-step confirmation by both parties before the transfer completes.
Buyer acceptance window: The buyer must accept the transfer within a few working days. One recent guide says 5 working days, while an older guide said 7 days, so check the current window on OneMotoring.
Transfer fee: LTA charges a transfer fee that the buyer pays online when accepting. A motoring guide put it at about $25, but the exact amount can be checked with the Enquire Transfer Fee service on OneMotoring.
Seller safety: LTA advises sellers to transfer the vehicle only after confirming that the money has arrived, especially for cheque payments. The buyer must accept the ownership transfer before the vehicle is handed over.
Outstanding loans: If the seller still has a car loan, the loan must be fully settled before the transfer can go through.
Insurance: The buyer needs motor insurance in the buyer's own name that is already in force at the time of transfer.
After transfer: Once the transfer completes, the buyer is responsible for fines and summonses linked to the vehicle from then on.
Checks before buying: Ask for the vehicle's inspection history if it is over 3 years old, the service records, the remaining COE period and the registration date. The remaining COE and PARF eligibility affect what the car is worth.
Dealer sales: A vehicle can be transferred temporarily to a motor dealer who then sells it on. This is done under the Temporary Transfer of Vehicle Scheme.
Used car price tip: A used car with fewer years of COE left costs less per year of use. Compare the price divided by the remaining COE months, and check whether the car can still be renewed for 5 or 10 years.
