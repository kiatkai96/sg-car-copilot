# COE (Certificate of Entitlement) explainer
Source URL: https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/buying/upfront-vehicle-costs/certificate-of-entitlement--coe-.html
Source page last updated by LTA: 6 October 2026
Date notes were written: 2026-11-08 (summarised in own words from the official OneMotoring page)

What a COE is: A Certificate of Entitlement (COE) is the right to own and use a vehicle in Singapore. Every vehicle needs one. A successful COE bid lets the vehicle be on the road for 10 years.
COE categories: There are five COE categories, A, B, C, D and E.
COE Category A: cars that are not fully electric, with an engine of up to 1,600cc and power of up to 97kW (130bhp). Fully electric cars with power of up to 110kW (147bhp) also count as Category A. The electric-car rule applies to COEs from the 1st bidding exercise of May 2022 onward.
COE Category B: cars that are not fully electric with an engine above 1,600cc or power above 97kW. Fully electric cars with power above 110kW (147bhp) are also Category B.
COE Category C: goods vehicles and buses. It can be transferred once if an individual bid for it.
COE Category D: motorcycles.
COE Category E: the open category, usable for any vehicle except motorcycles. It can be transferred once if an individual bid for it.
COE transfer: COEs in Categories A, B and D cannot be transferred. COEs bid for under a company, business or organisation cannot be transferred either.
COE bidding schedule: Bidding exercises are held twice a month. They usually start at 12 noon on the first and third Monday and run for three working days, closing at 4 pm on Wednesday when there is no public holiday in between.
How to bid: Individuals bid through DBS/POSB ATMs. Companies and motor traders bid through internet banking with DBS, UOB or Maybank.
How COE pricing works: Bidding is uniform-price. The Current COE Price is the highest unsuccessful bid plus $1. Every successful bidder in that category pays the same amount, called the Quota Premium (QP), which is the final Current COE Price when bidding closes.
COE pricing example: if the quota is 3 and the bids are $100, $88, $70, $70 and $41, the top two bids win and the Quota Premium is $71. The two $70 bids miss out because the quota is full.
Bid deposit: The bid deposit is $10,000 for Categories A, B, C and E. For Category D it is $1,500 for COEs from the 1st May 2023 exercise onward. Banks also charge an administration fee, usually $2 to $10.
Quota: The vehicle quota is updated every three months.
Temporary COE (TCOE): A successful bid gives a Temporary COE. The vehicle must be registered before the TCOE expires, otherwise the whole bid deposit is forfeited.
TCOE validity: 6 months for Categories A and B. 3 months for Categories C and E. 1 month for Category D TCOEs from the 1st May 2023 exercise onward.
COE renewal: At the end of the 10 years the owner can deregister the vehicle or renew the COE without bidding. Renewal costs the Prevailing Quota Premium (PQP), which is the moving average of COE prices over the last three months.
Deregistering early: If a vehicle is deregistered before its COE expires, a COE rebate for the unused period may be given, and a PARF rebate may also apply depending on the car's age.
Possible change: LTA has been running a public consultation on proposed changes to car COE categories, so category rules may change in future.
Where to find the latest COE prices: official bidding results are published by LTA, and the historical dataset "COE Bidding Results / Prices" is on data.gov.sg.
